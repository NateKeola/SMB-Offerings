# From observed friction to a scoped delivery

This process is a proposed operating synthesis. Existing source projects provide preparation tools and briefs; no client prototype or production implementation is completed by this handoff.

## 1. Discover before promising

Confirm the buyer's workflow and problem recognition; platform/version and ownership; existing tools; data rights and freshness; CMS/domain/hosting access; privacy boundaries; integration owners; capacity; constraints; baseline and acceptance criteria. Check whether a configuration change or conventional interface can solve the task before adding a model.

Catalog discovery needs authorized product/variant fields, supplier terms, permissions, customer-price separation and stale-data handling. A quote flow needs approved services, geography, eligibility, routing, response responsibility and error behavior. Do not infer available inventory, compatibility, quote values or appointments from suggestive text.

## 2. Build the smallest useful demonstration

Choose one task, a finite fixture set and a stop point. Use fictional or permissioned content, original assets and clearly marked rates. Keep live submission, purchasing and production integration disabled. Use a local artifact or protected preview only within the authorized scope; a localhost URL is not a durable public product link.

The brief must say what is already built, what behavior was checked, what remains simulated, what the buyer would need to supply, and what the next phase costs. Do not imply that a filmed reference or copied tutorial is our working implementation. Never fabricate testimonials or claim customer affiliation.

Test useful behavior: success and no-result states, errors, keyboard/mobile access, source attribution and freshness, hostile text, permission boundaries and recovery. A catalog prototype can compare exact and descriptive tasks using buyer-defined expected answers. A form prototype can use a dummy destination; testing a live receipt requires explicit client authorization.

## 3. Agree a statement of work

Use the [offer and SOW template](../templates/offer-and-sow.md). Specify:

- One outcome, evidence baseline and acceptance method, including unknowns and low-volume limits.
- Included pages, products/variants, suppliers, endpoints, locations, queries or revision rounds.
- Required access, client-supplied facts/assets, ownership, data handling and dependencies.
- Separate prototype, production integration, migration, deployment and ongoing care phases.
- Setup fee, recurring obligation, provider/usage costs, support cap, response target, change control and exit/export terms.
- Approval points, test destination, recovery/rollback plan and delivery dependencies.

Broad ERP, authentication, customer pricing, booking, payment or CRM changes need technical discovery before a fixed quote. Do not treat a prototype estimate as the entire production cost.

## 4. Accept and measure

Record evidence for each agreed requirement and unresolved exception. Website work should include the SEO toolkit's seven launch gates. Production changes and deployment require the customer's and user's relevant authorization; a successful test does not authorize launch by itself.

Separate implementation success from business impact. Proposed customer value is staff minutes saved × agreed time value plus incremental fulfilled work × contribution, minus incremental costs; keep components separate to avoid double counting. Traffic and submissions are intermediate metrics. A before/after change alone does not establish causation.

Where volumes permit, a thirty-day baseline and thirty-to-sixty-day follow-up may help; those are proposed measurement windows, not observed results or scheduled tasks. Record sample size, seasonality, ads, pricing changes and capacity. At low volume, report task success and delivery reliability instead of unsupported conversion uplift.

## 5. Maintain only what has a recurring obligation

Care should have finite checks, response expectations, support minutes and cost limits. Log actual labor, incidents and exceptions. Separate included work from upgrades and avoid double billing across care and add-ons. If the recurring benefit does not justify the price or effort, use a one-off engagement rather than forcing a subscription.

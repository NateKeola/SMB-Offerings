# Agency direction and open ICP questions

Recorded 2026-11-07. This is an original, public-safe synthesis of the latest business-planning conversation and the user's subsequent three-business field report. It is a working record, not a transcript, customer case study or finalized offer.

## Current direction from the planning conversation

- **User direction:** Build an AI implementation agency with a small menu of practical, repeatable improvements. Explain the customer task and business value before the technology.
- **Meeting direction:** Start discovery with wholesale garment distributors and a bounded semantic-search demonstration. The rationale is familiarity with the product-discovery workflow, not validated demand across the market.
- **Meeting direction:** Use direct customer conversations and an honest demonstration to learn what matters. In-person selling was discussed; this document does not authorize outreach or visits.
- **Delivery insight:** A convincing demonstration can be much easier than a reliable production integration. Catalog access, freshness, permissions, existing systems, monitoring and support need their own discovery and scope.
- **Working preference:** Build reusable capabilities where that makes sense. This is not a decision to rebuild every infrastructure service or a verified build-versus-buy conclusion.
- **Preparation still needed:** Finalize the name, website, scoped menu, preliminary pricing assumptions and demonstration boundaries. These are planning items, not completed deliverables.

The user is also investigating local-service businesses and wants to develop the ICP one question at a time. Wholesale is the meeting's initial direction; whether roofing or another service category replaces it, complements it or remains research is **open**.

## Three roofing calls: preliminary field evidence

**Source class: user-reported anecdote. Sample: three businesses, non-random and not independently verified.**

The user reported calling three roofing businesses:

| Anonymous observation | User's description | What is not established |
|---|---|---|
| Business A | Had an AI agent | Provider, technical architecture, coverage, quality and outcome |
| Business B | Had a text agent | Whether it was SMS, web chat or another flow; automation and quality |
| Business C | Had no agent and appeared farther from the first search-results page | Exact query, rank, surface, location, time and whether an agent existed on another channel |

Names, URLs, exact search terms, call times and detailed outcomes have not been supplied. Do not reconstruct them or turn these three rows into identifiable lead records.

**What this supports:** Different response setups were encountered in this small sample. A presence/absence field is useful when mapping the journey.

**What it does not support:** Market adoption rates, a representative pattern, poor service at Business C, lost revenue, willingness to pay, or a causal relationship between search position and agent adoption. Lower search visibility and lack of a detected agent are separate observations. Either can coexist with an effective business.

**User interpretation to test:** Some owners may already be implementing these tools themselves, so the value may be reliable implementation and workflow improvement rather than mere access to AI. The calls alone do not establish who implemented the systems.

## ICP questions to answer incrementally

Ask one question at a time during the working conversation; retain unknowns instead of silently filling them.

1. **First wedge:** Does wholesale remain the first focus, or are local-service businesses now the priority?
2. **Buyer and work:** Which owner/team and which recurring customer task are we trying to improve?
3. **Failure point:** In the actual journey, where did a customer get stuck or have to wait? What worked?
4. **Fit and capacity:** What company size, geography, job types and available capacity make the problem worth solving?
5. **Existing process:** What happens today across staff, phone, text, forms and scheduling? Who owns it?
6. **Value and measurement:** Which baseline and outcome could the buyer verify without invented revenue or savings?
7. **Delivery fit:** Which data, permissions and integrations are available, and what must remain human-controlled?
8. **Commercial test:** What bounded paid pilot and acceptance criteria would test willingness to pay?

No minimum company size, revenue, search rank, review count, budget or final geography has been selected here. See the [prospecting workflow](../research/prospecting-workflow.md) for a flexible way to preserve the user's actual pathway.

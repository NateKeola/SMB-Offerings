# ICP discovery cards

Updated 2026-11-07. **Proposed research cards**, not validated customer segments or promises that these businesses need AI. Use the [qualification gates](opportunity-qualification.md), [private worksheet](../templates/icp.md) and [observation workflow](../research/prospecting-workflow.md).

## Shared definition

An ICP is a buyer with a recurring task, a verified problem, a reason to act and a feasible delivery path. Industry and location narrow discovery; poor aesthetics, low search position and absent automation do not establish fit.

For every card, record: buyer role; task; business/service area; observed workflow; alternatives; pain frequency; capacity; data/system access; bounded improvement; baseline; acceptance test; disqualifiers; and unresolved questions. Ask the next missing question, not an entire survey at once. Budget and willingness to pay stay unknown until a credible source establishes them.

## Roofing inquiry and quote preparation

**Origin:** User-reported calls to three roofers and a request to learn intake, follow-up, quoting and website differences. This is a separate discovery track from the wholesale-first meeting direction.

| Dimension | Working hypothesis or required evidence |
|---|---|
| Buyer | Owner, office manager or estimator; verify who owns inquiry handling and can approve changes |
| Customer task | Determine repair/replacement/service eligibility, request an inspection or quote, and know what happens next |
| Compare like with like | Residential versus commercial, repair versus replacement, service area, emergency coverage and branch; record unknowns |
| Inspect first | Mobile call route, service-area clarity, useful quote CTA, required fields, error instructions, next-step expectations and stated hours |
| Candidate pain | Repeated navigation failure, unclear scope/routing or buyer-confirmed intake/estimation burden, not merely a missing chatbot |
| Low-integration offer | Repair the inquiry path and routing; reuse an adequate existing form/CRM rather than forcing a new agent |
| Conditional next offer | After-hours intake or owner notification only if the current process, capacity, permissions and handoff justify it |
| Later offer | Quote/RFQ draft from approved inputs and past-job rules, with estimator approval; no autonomous final price |
| Baseline | Owner-authorized qualified inquiries, response interval, inspection-booking completion, manual clarification work and routing errors |
| Pilot acceptance | Agreed test inquiries reach the right queue with usable details; no unsupported promises; human fallback and deletion tested |
| Disqualifiers | Good existing route with no buyer-recognized issue, no capacity, unsupported emergency/safety handling, inaccessible required systems or unbounded pricing liability |

### Walkthrough sequence

1. Record the actual search query, city/location context, date, device and surface. Ads, Maps and organic results are separate observations.
2. Confirm the business/domain/branch and one actual service. Open the homepage, service page and contact/quote path in the tested viewport.
3. Trace the shortest visible route to an appropriate next step. Record expected versus actual result and an alternative route that works, if any. Inspect without submitting.
4. Compare one strong example and one candidate for the same task. Keep both successful patterns and friction; do not assume a highly ranked site converts well.
5. Privately add what the user actually did by phone. Separate user-reported outcome from fresh browser evidence and any untested follow-up.
6. Choose one fact to verify with the buyer. A missing quote form can be intentional when a human inspection is essential.

### Buyer questions, in order

- What happens from a new inquiry to a booked inspection, and who owns each step?
- Which jobs and areas do you want more of, and which should be declined?
- Where do customers or staff most often get stuck? How do you know?
- What details are necessary before an estimator can act, and which must wait for an inspection?
- What already handles calls, SMS, scheduling and estimates? What would a small improvement need to integrate with?
- Which outcome could we measure during a bounded pilot, and who approves scope and pricing?

Do not diagnose roof condition, structural safety or an emergency from a prospecting interaction. Roof dimensions, pitch, imagery rights and price inference would require their own validated inputs and scope.

## Wholesale catalog discovery

**Origin:** Latest agency meeting's initial focus on garment wholesalers, with later interest in other wholesale categories. Familiarity with a workflow is not evidence that every wholesaler needs the same system.

| Dimension | Working hypothesis or required evidence |
|---|---|
| Buyer | Owner, sales operations, ecommerce lead or branch operations; verify authority and IT/data dependencies |
| Customer task | Find a known item or suitable product using codes, description and attributes, then prepare a useful RFQ |
| Compare like with like | Garment distributor versus plumbing, industrial or other supplier; catalog breadth, customer type, geography and purchasing model |
| Inspect first | Exact known-product control; manufacturer/distributor code difference; descriptive query; variant/filter handling; RFQ route |
| Candidate pain | Repeated buyer-task failure or buyer-confirmed rep lookup burden; absence of public search may be deliberate for account-only catalogs |
| First offer | Bounded product-discovery demo on permitted catalog data, with provenance and explicit demonstration limits |
| Baseline | Agreed task success, time to a useful result/RFQ, wrong-variant rate and rep clarification workload |
| Pilot acceptance | Held-out product tasks meet agreed criteria; known-item accuracy, variant correctness and honest unknowns; no invented stock/price |
| Integration discovery | Catalog/PIM/ERP source, update schedule, API/export permission, units, variants, pricing visibility and data owner |
| Disqualifiers | No reproducible task gap, no usable rights/data, unreliable identifiers, unsupported safety-critical substitutions or too much integration for the pilot |

### Scope ladder

1. **Catalog demo:** A sample, freshness-stamped search experience. Not a live copy of the customer's system or proof of production readiness.
2. **Read-only production search:** Authorized feed, access controls, incremental updates, evaluation, monitoring and fallback.
3. **RFQ/order draft:** Human reviews items, quantities, variants and customer-specific terms. No order submission.
4. **Inventory-aware response:** Authorized live source, freshness/error behavior and explicit unknown state. A cached catalog does not establish current stock.
5. **Backend order creation:** Separate authorization and implementation scope, validated pricing/permissions, idempotency, review, audit trail and recovery. Never implied by a successful search demo.

Do not assume products are equivalent solely from semantic similarity. For technical/safety-critical items, exact specifications and authorized human judgment remain necessary.

### Buyer questions, in order

- What do customers ask sales to find that the site cannot reliably help them find?
- Can you show a successful item lookup and a recent difficult one?
- Are the failures about language, identifiers, variants, unavailable stock, pricing access or the purchasing process?
- Which system is authoritative for each of those facts, and who controls access?
- What repeatable task would make a small search/RFQ pilot useful without changing live orders?
- What accuracy, support and commercial result would justify paying for the next stage?

## Expand only after comparing tasks

| Adjacent category | Reusable part | Must be rediscovered |
|---|---|---|
| HVAC, plumbing and electrical | Service-area/inquiry mapping and staff handoff | Emergency boundaries, dispatch capacity and trade-specific intake |
| Landscaping, cleaning and scheduled services | Quote/booking-route clarity | Recurrence, property scope, seasonality and existing scheduling tools |
| Non-garment wholesalers | Product task/control, evidence ledger and RFQ flow | Units, identifiers, substitutions, safety and ERP/pricing rules |
| Other established SMBs | Buyer interview, source-backed diagnosis and bounded pilot | The task and value case; construction is not a requirement |

## Choosing the next commercial wedge

Compare the tracks on verified pain, buyer access, data availability, small-pilot feasibility, measurable outcome and support burden. Record what is known and the single next test per criterion. Do not rank by estimated company revenue or hypothetical ROI alone. A public-site candidate can enter research before a buyer conversation, but cannot be described as buyer-validated or commercially qualified until that evidence exists.

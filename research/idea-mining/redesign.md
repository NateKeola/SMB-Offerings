# Rights-aware website redesign

## Framework

Qualify the task, inventory facts/assets, verify reuse rights, build a private concept, test it, then obtain launch approval. Public availability does not license photographs, copy, artwork, video, fonts or a distinctive design. A business may not own every asset on its existing site.

Maintain an asset ledger: source page/URL; type; owner/license evidence; intended use; attribution; approval status (approved, reference only, needs permission). Prefer owner-supplied originals, licensed stock, original visuals or clearly labeled placeholders.

Google Maps photos are not a default downloadable image library. Instagram research does not grant media rights. Use official embeds/APIs only with current rights, attribution and terms. Do not crop attribution or casually copy reviews. Verify approved testimonials and claims.

## Reusable redesign prompt

Act as a local-service website strategist/designer. Prepare a researched concept for [BUSINESS] at [OFFICIAL URL], serving [LOCATIONS], with primary goal [CALLS/APPOINTMENTS/QUALIFIED QUOTES]. Approved sources are [LINKS]; approved assets/permissions are [DETAILS]; platform, budget and maintenance constraints are [DETAILS].

1. Confirm identity and accessible first-party pages/profiles. Record dates and source URLs. Mark inaccessible sources accurately. Do not contact, sign in, buy, publish or bypass access restrictions.
2. Prepare a factual brief: services, audience, geography, tone, differentiation, existing next steps and trust evidence. Separate verified facts, conflicts and assumptions. Invent no prices, availability, licenses, staff, testimonials or outcomes.
3. Audit desktop/mobile journeys. Identify five consequential problems with page-level evidence. Separate measured performance from visual impressions.
4. Create page/asset inventory and rights ledger. Reuse only authorized assets, licensed material, original visuals or placeholders; do not scrape/rehost social or Maps media by default.
5. Recommend the simplest platform with editing, maintenance, accessibility, ownership/export, integration and recurring-cost implications. Framer or Webflow may suit marketing/CMS needs; Next.js may suit custom workflows but adds engineering obligations. Do not add a database just to label a brochure site dynamic.
6. Propose sitemap, conversion-focused wireframes and a distinctive visual direction. Use clear service/location information, one primary next step, credible proof, services, process, genuine project gallery, FAQ and contact. Dynamic features must solve a task, not merely animate.
7. Build only if separately authorized. Keep the concept private through actual access controls; noindex is supplementary, not privacy. Label independent concepts. Forms remain non-submitting and integrations in demo mode until destination, data handling and authorization are settled.
8. Use original copy and factual structured data. Preserve useful URLs and plan redirects. Avoid doorway pages, invented ratings and guaranteed SEO outcomes.
9. Test representative mobile/desktop widths, keyboard/focus, contrast, reduced motion, image size, layout shifts, links and interactions. Exercise authorized test forms through success/error/validation states. Report only tests run.
10. Deliver brief, evidence audit, rights ledger, sitemap, visual direction, authorized preview/files, missing approvals and launch checklist.

Stop before public launch, real data collection, paid services or external communications unless specifically authorized.

## Acceptance and handoff

Verify factual content and asset permissions; chosen URLs and redirects; correct contact/form delivery; privacy/analytics decisions; mobile and accessibility behavior; customer editing process; hosting/maintenance cost; ownership/export and cancellation. Do not present a non-submitting concept as a working booking service.

## Reference-inspired original video concepts

Photographs may be studied as visual inspiration rather than reused verbatim in a generated video. Record the reference source and extract general ideas such as lighting, framing, pacing or atmosphere; develop a distinct storyboard and use original or appropriately licensed production material. Do not imply the generated scene depicts real completed work or an actual customer unless verified and authorized.

Reference-only intent, a new medium or generative transformation does not itself establish copyright safety. Review rights and permissions before uploading source images to a third-party generation service; consider recognizable people, logos, protected expression, privacy, publicity and trademark risks. Avoid close recreation of distinctive protected material. Keep reference study, authorized model inputs and final commercial-use approval as separate gates.

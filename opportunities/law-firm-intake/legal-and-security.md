# Legal and security assessment

Research as of **2026-11-03**, California first. This is product-risk research, not a legal opinion on any person's matter or a comprehensive 50-state analysis. A firm and qualified counsel must resolve the actual implementation, jurisdictions and contracts before real intake.

## The important distinction

**A receptionist can avoid giving legal advice while still receiving protected information.** California [Rule 1.18](https://www.calbar.ca.gov/Portals/0/documents/rules/Rule_1.18-Exec_Summary-Redline.pdf) covers consultations about retaining a lawyer. Its confidentiality duties can apply even if no lawyer-client relationship follows. Its disqualification standard refers to protected information **material to the matter**; do not substitute the ABA Model Rule's “significantly harmful” formulation for California's language.

A short callback request reduces what is collected. It does not guarantee no prospective-client duties. A warning alone cannot establish that an invited, substantive AI intake conversation is unprotected. The actual invitation, reasonable expectations, information flow and firm conduct matter. Stop inviting detailed stories before the firm has determined what it can receive.

California [Formal Opinion 2021-205](https://www.calbar.ca.gov/sites/default/files/portals/0/documents/ethics/Opinions/Formal-Opinion-No-2021-205-Duties-to-Prospective-Client.pdf), advisory interpretation of Rule 1.18, supports limiting questions to information reasonably necessary for the representation decision and warning against unnecessary disclosure. The proposed callback-only tier is a deliberately narrower product choice, not a claim that lawyers may never ask more at an appropriate stage.

Privilege is distinct from the professional duty to protect information. California Evidence Code [951](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=EVID&sectionNum=951.) includes consultations to retain counsel; [952](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=EVID&sectionNum=952.) addresses confidential communications and necessary intermediaries; [954](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=EVID&sectionNum=954.) provides the privilege. Do not promise every bot conversation is privileged, or assume vendor involvement always destroys privilege. Counsel must assess purpose, agency, disclosure and contracts. A portal can contain sensitive information regardless of privilege.

## Broader US distinctions

**Enacted future requirement to plan for:** [SB 574's chaptered text](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billTextClient.xhtml?bill_id=202520260SB574) and [history](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billHistoryClient.xhtml?bill_id=202520260SB574) confirm Chapter 858, Statutes of 2026, approved September 30, 2026. New BPC 6068.1 prohibits delegating the practice of law to generative AI; limits input of confidential, identifying and other nonpublic information where access is not restricted to the attorney and authorized persons under confidentiality obligations; and requires reasonable verification/correction of outputs. It also addresses court-document disclosure and considering disclosure for public content. Phone numbers of parties are among its listed identifying information, so minimal intake is not necessarily outside its scope.

**Timing:** the reviewed final act contains no urgency or special effective-date clause. Applying [California Constitution article IV, section 8(c)](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CONS&sectionNum=SEC.%208.&article=IV), the expected effective date is **January 1, 2027**; the [California Courts October 1 notice](https://newsroom.courts.ca.gov/news/lawyers-face-stronger-ai-review-responsibilities-under-new-california-law) likewise describes requirements starting next year. This date is a legal-text inference, not a claim that the new section was already operative on November 3, 2026. Recheck with counsel before launch. Existing confidentiality/competence duties already apply. The statute does not itself ban all AI-assisted administrative intake or establish that a particular vendor meets the access restrictions.

[ABA Formal Opinion 512 (July 29, 2024)](https://www.americanbar.org/content/dam/aba/administrative/professional_responsibility/ethics-opinions/aba-formal-opinion-512.pdf) is persuasive model-rule guidance, not nationwide binding law. It addresses confidentiality, competence, communication, supervision and informed consent for relevant AI use. Local adopted rules control.

[Florida Opinion 24-1](https://www.floridabar.org/etopinions/opinion-24-1/) is expressly advisory. It discusses factual intake, disclosure that the chatbot is not a lawyer or firm employee, and the risk of unintended prospective/client relationships. [Oregon Opinion 2026-208](https://www.osbar.org/_docs/ethics/2026-208.pdf) specifically covers chatbot/AI-agent communications, prospective-client conflicts and supervision of autonomous actions. These support careful design; they do not license the same flow in every state.

Before expanding, counsel should build a jurisdiction matrix for professional rules, UPL, recording/interception, AI disclosures/automated decisions, consumer privacy, breach, retention and communication rules. The present review cannot certify a US-wide service.

## Minimum operational security, even for callback-only

These are **proposed controls**, not a claim of certification or a universal statutory checklist:

- Prefer the firm's existing approved CRM/intake portal. Use MFA, least-privilege roles, tenant and matter separation, access revocation, audit events and encryption in transit/at rest. An unlisted public URL or shared spreadsheet link is not access control.
- Map telephony, speech recognition, model, email, integration, storage, monitoring, backup and human-review subprocessors. Verify purpose limits, training use, retention, geography, deletion, staff access, incident notice and export/exit terms. A vendor's security badge does not prove this particular configuration is suitable.
- Use a dedicated new-inquiry mailbox with the narrowest permissions. Do not grant the agent access to all partner mail or historic matters. Suppress sensitive content from notification subjects, analytics, logs and error trackers.
- Default to no retained raw audio/transcript unless the firm identifies a justified purpose and approved consent/retention policy. Transient processing still counts as processing; backups and vendor logs need review. Retain the minimum task record; preserve necessary conflict records/legal holds on a separately approved schedule. No invented universal 30-day deletion rule.
- For a later attachment tier: signed expiring links, restricted file types/sizes, malware quarantine, no automatic link opening or macro execution, isolated extraction, prompt-injection defenses and human release. Unexpected documents stay quarantined; do not silently destroy potentially important material.
- Maintain incident contacts, a tested disable/fallback path, reconciliation of failed deliveries, backup restoration and deletion evidence. Separate deployment authorization from document approval.

## Risk matrix and residual uncertainty

Severity is a planning judgment, not a calculated legal probability.

| Failure | Initial severity | Control / accountable owner | Residual question or launch condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| Protected narrative collected before conflict check | High | Minimal script, interrupt/redirect narrative, restricted queue; firm counsel | Can this firm receive this field at this stage? |
| Agent gives advice or rejects a viable matter | High | Allowlisted administrative content, no merits score, human decision; supervising lawyer | Validate adversarial and ordinary cases before live use |
| Recording/transcription begins before lawful consent | High | Pre-capture flow plus human alternative; counsel and telephony operator | Prove vendor behavior including buffers, logs and transfers |
| Cross-firm disclosure or wrong-recipient email | Critical | Isolation, recipient validation, scoped tokens, no case text in notifications; security owner | Independent access-control and integration tests |
| Caller believes firm accepted representation | High | Plain limitations, controlled scheduling text, no autonomous engagement; lawyer | Counsel-approved scripts and human review of edge cases |
| Deadline/emergency is buried | High | Flag self-reported urgency immediately, accessible escalation, retry ownership; intake manager | Named staffed coverage and what happens when nobody answers |
| Hallucinated summary, misspelled party or wrong contact | High | Read-back, source-linked fields, missingness labels and human corrections; intake staff | Never treat contact-channel confirmation as identity verification |
| Harmful attachment or prompt injection | High | No uploads in MVP; quarantined extraction later; security owner | Documents cannot instruct tool use, disclosure or approval |
| No human follow-through despite automatic acknowledgement | High | Owner, due time, overdue alerts and completion evidence; office manager | Capacity and accountable callback service level required |
| Discrimination or inaccessible routing | High | No protected-class/proxy merit scoring; alternate channels; lawyer and product owner | Review criteria and accessibility with affected user scenarios |
| Vendor terms/price/model changes | Medium–high | Versioned procurement review, monitoring, regression tests and exit plan; service owner | Revalidate before significant changes; do not silently switch model/data policy |

## Questions for counsel before any real-data pilot

1. What counts as a consultation in the exact voice/email flow, and which identifiers may be collected before conflicts review?
2. What disclaimer, AI disclosure, recording/transcription consent and declined-consent alternative are valid for expected caller locations?
3. What vendor/subprocessor access preserves confidentiality and, where applicable, privilege? What informed consent is required for the chosen tools?
4. Which privacy, security, breach, retention, accessibility, insurance and contractual obligations apply to this firm and service provider?
5. Which acknowledgements, booking messages, document requests, rejection/referral messages and emergency/deadline escalations may run without individual review?
6. Does the proposed compensation/marketing model raise fee-sharing, referral, advertising or lawyer-independence concerns?

No counsel review has occurred. These are explicit unresolved prerequisites, not an automatic approval request or a claim that the opportunity is prohibited.

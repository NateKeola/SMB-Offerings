# Source register and research limitations

All sources checked **2026-11-03** unless a publication/update date is separately stated. Public source pages are linked; no third-party PDFs, reports, screenshots or private correspondence are rehosted. Claims in the other documents are original summaries.

## Method

Four research angles: responsiveness evidence, products/pricing, California and US legal duties, and delivery/economics. Exa searches requested **115 result slots across 21 queries**, followed by targeted read-only page reads. Those are search-result slots, including duplicates and rejected pages, **not 115 independently verified studies**. Primary legal authorities and first-party product documentation were preferred; commercial motives are disclosed. One alternate web reader was used when a privacy-policy extraction was incomplete, and it did not resolve the gap.

Search-result inclusion is not validation. Repeated Clio statistics on secondary sites were traced to the original report rather than counted as corroborating datasets. No buyer interviews, outreach, product signups, trial calls, paid APIs or raw study-data audits occurred. Current web pages can change; a checked date does not establish a permanent offer or legal opinion.

## Known retrieval and coverage gaps

The initially tried Answering Legal `/pricing` route was unavailable; official cost/help pages supplied the cited service information. Ruby/LawDroid pricing extraction did not preserve every tier label; unknowns remain explicit. Smith.ai privacy-policy text could not be verified. A California 2020-203 opinion link returned a landing page rather than the opinion text; it is not a basis for a substantive legal claim here. An old Rule 5.4 executive-summary PDF was marked superseded; the current chapter 5 text was then read and used instead. Public vendor claims do not prove data terms, security architecture or outcome gains.

No court-case survey resolves the application of interception/privilege law to this exact architecture. No full inventory of state AI/privacy/recording rules was completed. No independent 2026 representative national response-rate study or reliable total addressable market was established. These gaps are concrete next research questions, not evidence that a viable compliant service cannot be built.

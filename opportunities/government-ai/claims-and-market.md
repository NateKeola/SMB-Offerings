## Market size: keep the denominators

Brookings reports $7.2 billion obligated and $91.8 billion in potential awards in its 2026-labelled analysis. Its keyword-selected contracts and unequal collection windows cannot establish a clean annual market size. Its reported 98.9% defense concentration is a share of **potential value**, not obligations. The raw Leadership Connect dataset was not independently reconstructed.

[CSIS's September 2026 analysis](https://www.csis.org/analysis/understanding-us-federal-governments-ai-spending/) reports $1.162 billion of FY2025 obligations and $4.104 billion across FY2019–FY2025 selected AI-related awards/orders. Small businesses account for 70% of awards by count and 45% of obligations in that sample; defense accounts for 83% of obligations. Its appendix excludes grants, OTAs, subcontracts and parent vehicles, retains negative obligation adjustments, and warns about omissions and mixed-purpose purchases. Business size is distinct from set-aside status. These are not interchangeable with Brookings' cohorts or an estimate of addressable novice-agency revenue.

**Inference:** There is evidenced procurement activity and participation by smaller vendors, alongside concentrated spend and significant domain requirements. Neither dataset proves easy entry. No defensible national SAM or SOM for a new training/document-workflow service was calculated. Build it from eligible notices, demonstrated tasks and reachable buyers, not a percentage of the headline billions.

## Buyer segments and first-offer hypotheses

| Segment / buyer role | Proposed wedge | Evidence needed | Principal barrier |
|---|---|---|---|
| Established prime, program manager or small-business liaison | Fixed work package for workflow evaluation, documentation or training | Current task order, missing capability, named deliverable and workshare budget | Existing team, flow-downs, past performance and payment terms |
| City/county department, HR or IT lead | Role-specific AI training using already approved tools | Training gap beyond free courses; local purchasing route; measured learning goals | Local rules, incumbent training providers, procurement timing |
| Civilian federal program + contracting officer + security/data owner | Public-document retrieval or classification evaluation in an approved environment | A task baseline, funded requirement, access/vehicle route and accepted risk boundary | Security authorization, accessibility, data rights and integration |
| Government-funded workforce intermediary | Tailored training for a defined population | Provider criteria, geography, curriculum evidence and funded follow-on | An RFI/provider pool may carry no purchase commitment |
| Defense, clinical, benefits or other high-impact use | No initial offer proposed | Specialist capability, controls and program-specific authorization | Material mission/domain requirements exceed current evidence |

## Competition and substitutes

| Alternative | Verified signal | Implication |
|---|---|---|
| Free public-sector learning | [InnovateUS](https://innovate-us.org/course/responsible-ai-for-public-professionals/) provides free practical AI courses, assessments and materials. | Generic introductory slides have weak differentiation. Test contextual exercises, supervised adoption and measurable task performance. |
| Enterprise platforms / government agreements | [GSA's September 2026 OneGov announcement](https://www.gsa.gov/about-gsa/newsroom/news-releases/gsa-expands-onegov-ai-offerings-with-discounted-openais-chatgpt-09102026) describes discounted consumption-based access with training resources, expected from October 2026. | Model access itself is weak differentiation. Confirm the exact order and environment; do not reuse expired promotional pricing. |
| Large integrators | Brookings identifies established vendors such as Booz Allen, Palantir and Accenture Federal Services. | Compete on a bounded gap, or partner; generic AI implementation is already supplied. |
| Specialist government-cloud implementers | [Puget Sound AI](https://pugetsoundai.com/subcontracting/) markets government-cloud workflow and training capability. These are vendor claims, not independently audited performance. | Relevant tenant expertise, evidence and procurement readiness matter more than a fast demo. |
| Existing process or non-AI automation | Buyer-specific; not yet observed | Compare search, forms, rules and existing licensed tools before adding a model. |

The low-integration entry hypothesis is tailored training plus a measured workflow evaluation. A public-facing chatbot or live document-routing system has a larger acceptance and support burden. No evidence yet establishes willingness to pay for any of these offers.

# Procurement path and practical validation

Prepared 2026-11-07. Proposed steps; none of the registrations, submissions or customer actions below has been executed.

## Entry steps

1. **Choose one deliverable.** Example: a role-specific training workshop plus scored exercises, or a public-document retrieval/classification evaluation. Define the human task, baseline, output and exclusions. Avoid a general promise to automate an agency.
2. **Check business readiness privately.** Legal entity, relevant experience, capacity, banking/tax information, insurance, actual qualifications and delivery location. Never publish these records in this repository.
3. **Choose the buying route.** Direct federal work, subcontracting, local procurement and a workforce-provider pool are different channels. A grant funds assistance; a procurement buys goods/services. An RFI/sources-sought notice gathers information and normally does not award work. Follow the specific notice.
4. **Prepare federal registration when a direct bid justifies it.** Use [SAM's official process](https://sam.gov/entity-registration) and All Awards registration for procurement; UEI-only is insufficient for direct federal awards. Registration and renewal are free, and active registration requires renewal every 365 days. Allow at least ten business days after submission and additional validation time. Obtain no paid intermediary by default.
5. **Select truthful classifications.** Candidate codes from the carousel: 541511 custom computer programming; 541512 systems design; 541519 other computer related services; 541611 administrative/general management consulting; 611430 professional/management development training. Verify the current [Census definitions](https://www.census.gov/naics/) and solicitation's NAICS/size standard; do not claim SBA program certification without eligibility and approval.
6. **Build a capability statement.** One specific outcome, relevant examples and honest company qualifications. Separate founders' prior experience from company past performance. Describe team roles, evaluation method and limitations. Use only public or authorized demo data.
7. **Research awards and demand.** Use USAspending for historical prime awards and obligations; SAM for notices and amendments; agency forecasts for anticipated procurement; [SBA](https://www.sba.gov/counseling/prime-and-subcontracting/) for prime/subcontract routes. A forecast is not a solicitation; a historical award is not open revenue.
8. **Qualify before writing a bid.** Read the complete notice, attachments and amendments. Verify due time/time zone, vehicle access, set-aside eligibility, scope, performance location, data classification, staffing, references, evaluation criteria, pricing format and submission method.
9. **Use free assistance.** [APEX Accelerators](https://www.apexaccelerators.us/) provide no-cost government-contracting guidance. A readiness review may be more useful than a course purchase.
10. **Only then prepare a scoped proposal and delivery plan.** Clarify acceptance, permitted tools/environment, ownership, accessibility, support, changes and payment. Sending, signing, supplier registration and paid work remain separate actions.

## Thresholds and procurement traps

[Acquisition.gov](https://www.acquisition.gov/threshold-changes) confirms general thresholds of $15,000 micro-purchase and $350,000 simplified acquisition effective October 1, 2025. Special categories use other amounts. Service Contract Labor Standards applicability can reduce the micro threshold to $2,500. Local governments do not automatically adopt federal thresholds.

[FAR 13.2](https://www.acquisition.gov/far/subpart-13.2) prefers purchase cards for micro-purchases; it does not compel a buyer to buy from a particular supplier. [FAR 4.1102](https://www.acquisition.gov/far/4.1102) has a narrow SAM exception when the card is both the purchasing and payment mechanism below the threshold. Do not generalize this to all small contracts. [FAR 13.003](https://www.acquisition.gov/far/13.003) prohibits splitting requirements to avoid thresholds. Price for a real independent scope, never to evade competition.

[GSA GWACs](https://www.gsa.gov/technology/it-contract-vehicles-and-purchasing-programs/gwacs) are precompeted contracts with eligible holders. SAM registration alone does not grant access to their task orders. Check applicable agency Revolutionary FAR Overhaul deviations and the controlling solicitation before relying on general FAR text.

## Delivery feasibility

A workshop with public exercises can be delivered without creating a production system. For a retrieval/classification pilot, use a buyer-approved environment, source-linked answers or labels, abstention, human review and an exportable evaluation report. Start with public, non-sensitive documents and no live record changes.

[GSA's AI policy](https://www.gsa.gov/directives-library/accelerating-responsible-use-of-artificial-intelligence-at-gsa) and [OMB M-25-22](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/M-25-22-Driving-Efficient-Acquisition-of-Artificial-Intelligence-in-Government.pdf) show why performance, data rights, interoperability and acquisition governance matter. Requirements depend on agency/use case; not every pilot needs the same controls. A vendor's FedRAMP status does not authorize every custom application or agency use. Verify the exact service, authorization boundary, deployment and agency approval. The special [FedRAMP AI prioritization initiative](https://www.fedramp.gov/ai/) ended in April 2026; do not pitch it as an open shortcut.

Before production, resolve access controls, retention, records handling, privacy, accessibility, incident handling, data/IP ownership, approved subprocessors, support responsibility and model-change behavior. For CUI/defense scope, investigate the applicable clauses separately; generic SMB tooling does not establish readiness.

## Four-week learning experiment

The following caps and thresholds are proposed operating choices, not evidence of market conversion.

| Stage | Work and cap | Evidence / exit |
|---|---|---|
| Week 1: focus | Review up to ten source records and two full notice packages; cap discovery at eight person-hours | One viable task, two source-supported routes, explicit holds and a readiness checklist |
| Week 2: validate | Prepare a one-page capability statement and five buyer/partner interview briefs | Questions on actual task, approved tools, budget owner, procurement route, timeline and acceptance; conversations only if separately authorized |
| Week 3: demonstrate | Build one bounded public/synthetic demonstration only if the discovery evidence supports it | Training: scored before/after exercises. Document workflow: at least 30 held-out examples, source traceability, error taxonomy, abstention and human time measured |
| Week 4: decide | Price a fixed deliverable and review cost/time/fit | Continue only if a reachable buyer confirms the problem and route, delivery is supportable, and a credible paid-pilot discussion exists |

No unsolicited testing against live government systems. No upload of government nonpublic data to a consumer model. No automatic decisions on public benefits, medical care, enforcement or military operations. These are delivery scope choices for the initial offer.

For a document prototype, agree task-specific thresholds before testing: useful result rate, wrong-answer severity, classification precision/recall, staff correction time and cost per completed task. Do not invent a universal accuracy promise. For training, compare equivalent tasks with the same approved tools and record missing outcomes. An attractive demo is not a measured productivity result.

Stop or narrow the offer if free training already solves the problem, no purchasing route exists, the requirement is already satisfied, the notice is closed, the scope needs unverified credentials, or preparation cost makes the economics implausible.
